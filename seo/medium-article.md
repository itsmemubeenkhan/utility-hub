Title: I Built 12 Free Finance Calculators. Then I Translated Them Into Spanish.

## Draft

Most finance calculators feel like they were designed to sell you something. Popups, email gates, "talk to an advisor" buttons everywhere.

So I built the opposite: UtilityHub — 12 free calculators (mortgage, home affordability, debt payoff, compound interest, salary paycheck, retirement…) with the math shown openly and zero signup. The guides next to them explain *why* the numbers work, in plain English.

Then came the interesting part. The US has 60+ million Spanish speakers, and almost nobody builds quality Spanish-language finance tools for them. So I translated the entire experience — all 12 calculators, FAQs, and key guides — into neutral US Spanish: https://www.theutilityhub.online/es

Three things I learned:

**1. Translation is not localization.** "Down payment" becomes "pago inicial," but the real work was re-checking that every financial concept made sense for the audience — FHA loans, PMI, 401(k)s are US-specific systems that need explaining, not just translating.

**2. URL structure matters for SEO.** English keeps clean URLs (/tools/mortgage-calculator); Spanish lives under /es/ (/es/tools/calculadora-de-hipoteca), with bidirectional hreflang so Google knows they're equivalents, not duplicates.

**3. Free tools earn trust that content alone can't.** A calculator that answers "can I afford this house?" in 30 seconds does more for a reader than 2,000 words of advice.

If you're house-hunting, killing debt, or just curious what compound interest does over 30 years — it's free, no account: https://www.theutilityhub.online
