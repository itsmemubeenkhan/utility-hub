If **you make $300K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$1,028,000-$1,107,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$1,142,000-$1,384,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 300k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $1,028,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $1,107,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$300,000 ÷ 12 = **$25,000/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $25,000 × 0.28 = **$7,000/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $25,000 × 0.36 = **$9,000/month**

That $7,000 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $7,000/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $7,000 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$1,107,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $7,000 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $6,500 → **≈ $1,028,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$1,028K-$1,107K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $9,000/month, but on $300K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $2,000 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $8,000, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$1,028,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $7,500, still above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$1,028,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** back-end cap is $7,000, exactly at your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$1,028,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,500 → mortgage drops to roughly **$949,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,000 → mortgage roughly **$870,000**
- **$3,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,500 → mortgage roughly **$791,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 300k salary with debts** precisely, here are six debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|

| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $7,000 | ~$1,028,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $7,000 | ~$1,028,000 |

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $300K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $300,000 ÷ 26 = **$11,538.46**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $7,000 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$3,230.77**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $1,028,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$3,000.00** (the rest of the $3,230.77 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $300K, your housing budget is **$3,230.77 per paycheck** ($11,538.46 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $1,028,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $3,000.00 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Does a $1,500 monthly car payment reduce what I can borrow on $300K?
No. The back-end cap with $1,500 in debts is $7,500 a month, still above your $7,000 front-end budget, so your borrowing power stays at about **$1,028,000**. Debts only start shrinking it once they pass about $2,000 a month.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
