Car dealers will happily sell you a $45,000 truck on a $60,000 salary. Your budget will not be happy about it. Here is how to calculate what you can actually afford before you walk onto the lot.

## The rules that keep you safe

Two guidelines dominate car affordability advice:

**The 15% rule:** Spend no more than **15% of your monthly take-home pay** on total car costs, payment plus insurance plus fuel. On $60,000 salary, take-home is roughly $3,750–$4,000/month after taxes (varies by state), so 15% gives you **$560–$600/month** for everything car-related.

**The 20/4/10 rule:** Put **20% down**, finance for no more than **4 years**, and keep total car expenses under **10% of gross income**. On $60,000, that is $500/month max for the payment itself.

Both rules point to the same neighborhood. Use the stricter one as your ceiling.

## What that buys you

Working backward from a $550/month all-in budget:

- Insurance on a financed car: ~$150–$200/month for most drivers
- Fuel and maintenance: ~$100–$150/month
- **Leftover for the payment: ~$250–$300/month**

At 7% APR over 48 months (the 20/4/10 term limit), a $275/month payment supports roughly an **$11,500 loan**. With 20% down, that means a car priced around **$14,000–$15,000**.

That number shocks people, and it should. It is the *responsible* number. Stretching to a 72-month term to "afford" a $25,000 car is exactly how people end up underwater, owing more than the car is worth for years.

## The costs everyone forgets

The payment is only part of ownership. Budget for all of it:

- **Insurance:** Required to be comprehensive on financed cars, often double the cost of liability-only.
- **Depreciation:** New cars lose roughly 20% in year one. A 2–3 year old car dodges the steepest drop.
- **Maintenance and repairs:** Budget $100/month minimum; tires, brakes, and surprises are not optional.
- **Registration and taxes:** Varies by state but can add thousands upfront.

## New vs used on $60k

On this salary, **a 2–4 year old used car is almost always the right answer**. You skip the worst depreciation, insurance is cheaper, and the payment fits the rules above. Buy new only if you can pay cash or the payment genuinely fits inside the 15% rule, which on $60,000 it rarely does for new cars.

## The bottom line

On $60,000, target roughly **$14,000–$18,000** for the car price with 20% down and a 4-year loan. It is not glamorous, but neither is a $600/month payment strangling your ability to save, invest, or handle emergencies.
