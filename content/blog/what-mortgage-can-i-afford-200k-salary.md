If **you make $200K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$659,000-$738,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$732,000-$824,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 200k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $659,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $738,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$200,000 ÷ 12 = **$16,666.67/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $16,666.67 × 0.28 = **$4,666.67/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $16,666.67 × 0.36 = **$6,000/month**

That $4,666.67 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $4,666.67/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $4,666.67 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$738,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $4,666.67 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $4,166.67 → **≈ $659,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$659K-$738K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $6,000/month:

- **No debts:** full $4,666.67 housing budget → ~$659K-$738K mortgage
- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $5,000, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$659,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $4,500 → mortgage drops to roughly **$633,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $4,000 → mortgage roughly **$554,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $3,500 → mortgage roughly **$475,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 200k salary with debts** precisely, here are four debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $4,666.67 | ~$659,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $4,500 | ~$633,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $4,000 | ~$554,000 |
| $2,500 (car + loans + cards) | $3,500 | ~$475,000 |

At $2,500 a month in debts your borrowing power is roughly 72% of what it is with no debts. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator) before you start house-hunting.

## What about a 15-year mortgage?

On $200K, a 15-year loan is tight but not impossible. At 6.0%, $659,000 over 15 years costs **$5,562.80/month** in P&I alone - already over your $4,666.67 housing budget before taxes and insurance. A smaller 15-year loan of about $553,000 fits ($4,666.67 P&I at 6.0%), but most buyers are better off with the 30-year term and extra principal payments when they can.

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $200K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $200,000 ÷ 26 = **$7,692.31**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $4,666.67 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$2,153.85**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $659,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$1,923.08** (the rest of the $2,153.85 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $200K salary?
Roughly a **$659,000 to $738,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $4,666.67 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford an $800K house on a $200K salary?
Yes. With 20% down, an $800,000 home costs about **$4,545.24 a month** including taxes and insurance, $121.43 under the $4,666.67 budget. With 10% down it costs about $5,350.89 a month, $684.22 over the guideline.

### Can I afford an $850K house on a $200K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, an $850,000 home costs about **$4,798.06 a month** including taxes and insurance, $131.40 over the $4,666.67 budget. You would need roughly a **$206,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### Can I afford a $900K house on a $200K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $900,000 home costs about **$5,050.89 a month** including taxes and insurance, $384.22 over the $4,666.67 budget. You would need roughly a **$216,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### What mortgage can I afford on $200K salary with debts?
With $1,000 a month in debts, about **$659,000** (the back-end cap stays above the front-end budget). With $1,500 a month in debts, about **$633,000**. With $2,000 a month in debts, about **$554,000**. With $2,500 a month in debts, about **$475,000**. All figures are after taxes and insurance at 6.5% on a 30-year loan.

### How much house can I afford on $200K with 20% down?
About an **$824,000 home**: a $659,000 loan costs $4,166.67 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $4,666.67 a month, right at the 28% guideline. An $800,000 home with 20% down is $121.43 a month under.

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $200,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$4,666.67/month** for housing, supporting about a **$659,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $738,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $200K, your housing budget is **$2,153.85 per paycheck** ($7,692.31 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $659,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $1,923.08 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
