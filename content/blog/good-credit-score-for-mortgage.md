Your credit score does not just determine *whether* you get a mortgage, it determines *how much* you pay for it every month for decades. A 40-point difference in score can cost or save you tens of thousands in interest. Here is where you stand.

## Minimum scores by loan type

Lenders set floors, and they differ by program:

- **Conventional loan:** 620 minimum at most lenders. Below that, you will generally need a government-backed program.
- **FHA loan:** 580 for 3.5% down; 500–579 possible with 10% down (though few lenders actually go that low).
- **VA loan:** No official minimum from the VA itself, but most lenders require 580–620.
- **USDA loan:** No official minimum, but lenders typically want 640+.

Meeting the minimum gets you *approved*. It does not get you a *good rate*.

## Where the real savings start

Mortgage pricing tiers reward higher scores in steps. The biggest jumps happen around these lines:

- **760+:** Best rates. This is the top tier at most lenders.
- **740–759:** Nearly the best, often within an eighth of a point of top-tier pricing.
- **700–739:** Good rates, but you are paying a noticeable premium versus 760+.
- **680–699:** You will feel it, roughly a quarter to half point higher than top tier.
- **620–679:** Approved, but expensive. The rate premium here is real money.

On a $400,000 loan, the difference between a 7.0% rate (good credit) and 7.5% (fair credit) is about **$130/month**, or roughly **$47,000** over 30 years. That is the price of a mediocre score, paid monthly for three decades.

## How to improve your score before applying

If you are 6–12 months from buying, these moves have the highest return:

1. **Pay down revolving balances.** Credit utilization, your balances relative to limits, is the fastest lever. Getting utilization under 30% helps; under 10% is ideal.
2. **Do not close old cards.** Length of credit history matters, and closing your oldest card shortens your average account age.
3. **Do not open new credit.** Every application is a hard inquiry, and new accounts lower your average age. Freeze new applications for at least 6 months before mortgage shopping.
4. **Dispute genuine errors.** Wrong late payments or accounts that are not yours can often be removed, sometimes worth dozens of points.
5. **Become an authorized user** on a family member's old, clean card if your own history is thin.

Avoid "rapid rescore" gimmicks and anyone promising to delete accurate negative items. They cannot.

## Rate shopping without hurting your score

Multiple mortgage inquiries within a focused window, typically 14 to 45 days depending on the scoring model, count as **a single inquiry** for scoring purposes. So shop aggressively: get quotes from at least 3–5 lenders. The rate difference between lenders is often larger than people expect, and comparing is free.

## The bottom line

620 gets you in the door; 740+ gets you the good rates; 760+ gets you the best. If you are close to a tier boundary, even 20 points can be worth thousands, so it pays to wait and polish your score before you lock.
