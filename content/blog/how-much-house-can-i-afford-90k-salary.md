$90,000 puts you comfortably above the US median household income, and it opens up most suburban markets in the country. Using the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule**, the answer lands around **$310k–$345k** depending on your debts, down payment and rate. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income

On $90,000/year ($7,500/month):

- Front-end limit: $7,500 × 0.28 = **$2,100/month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $7,500 × 0.36 = $2,700/month for everything

If you have $550/month in car and student loan payments, the back-end rule allows $2,700 − $550 = $2,150 for housing, so the front-end $2,100 is the binding limit.

## Converting the payment to a price

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $2,100/month in principal and interest supports a loan of about **$332,000**. With 20% down, that means a home price of roughly **$415,000**, but wait. That $2,100 must also cover property taxes and insurance (PITI), which lenders include in "housing." After ~$360/month for taxes and insurance, the P&I budget is closer to $1,741, supporting a loan of ~$275,000 and a price of **~$345,000** with 20% down.

Realistic ranges on $90K salary:

| Down payment | Approx. max home price (6.5%, 30-yr) |
|---|---|
| 10% down | ~$310,000 |
| 20% down | ~$345,000 |
| Debts over $900/mo | subtract ~$50,000 |

## Five things that move the number

1. **Interest rate.** At 5.5% instead of 6.5%, the same payment buys ~12% more house. Rate matters more than most buyers think.
2. **Existing debts.** Every $200/month in debt payments cuts roughly $30,000 off your max price. Killing a car payment before buying is powerful.
3. **Down payment.** At this income, 20% down is within reach for most buyers within a few years of saving — and it removes PMI (about 0.5–1% of the loan per year) while lowering the monthly payment from day one.
4. **Property taxes.** In New Jersey or Illinois, taxes can add $700+/month on homes in this range; in Texas, expect ~$600; in Alabama, under $200. Location changes affordability enormously.
5. **HOA dues.** A $300/month HOA fee counts against your ratios exactly like a higher mortgage payment.

## The costs buyers forget

The mortgage is only the headline. Budget ~1% of the home's value per year for maintenance ($3,500/year on a $345,000 home), plus utilities, and an emergency fund that covers 3–6 months of the *new* higher housing cost, not your old rent. At this price point, don't skip the home inspection to save $500 — it's the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.

## Should you max it out?

The 28/36 rule is a **ceiling**, not a target. Buying 10–20% below your max leaves room for savings, repairs and life. Being "house poor", technically approved but with zero margin, is how a good salary turns into chronic financial stress.
