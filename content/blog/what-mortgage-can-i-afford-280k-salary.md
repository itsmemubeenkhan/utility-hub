If **you make $280K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$955,000-$1,034,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$990,000-$1,293,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 280k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $955,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $1,034,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$280,000 ÷ 12 = **$23,333.33/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $23,333.33 × 0.28 = **$6,533.33/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $23,333.33 × 0.36 = **$8,400/month**

That $6,533.33 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $6,533.33/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $6,533.33 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$1,034,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $6,533.33 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $6,033.33 → **≈ $955,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$955K-$1,034K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $8,400/month, but on $280K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $1,867 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $7,400, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$955,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $6,900, still just above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$955,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,400 → mortgage drops to roughly **$933,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,900 → mortgage roughly **$854,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,400 → mortgage roughly **$775,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 280k salary with debts** precisely, here are five debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $6,533.33 | ~$955,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $6,533.33 | ~$955,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $6,400 | ~$933,000 |
| $2,500 (car + loans + cards) | $5,900 | ~$854,000 |
| $3,000 (car + loans + cards) | $5,400 | ~$775,000 |

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $280K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $280,000 ÷ 26 = **$10,769.23**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $6,533.33 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$3,015.38**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $955,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$2,784.62** (the rest of the $3,015.38 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?

### Does a $1,500 monthly car payment reduce what I can borrow on $280K?
No. The back-end cap with $1,500 in debts is $6,900 a month, still above your $6,533.33 front-end budget, so your borrowing power stays at about **$955,000**. Debts only start shrinking it once they pass about $1,867 a month.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
