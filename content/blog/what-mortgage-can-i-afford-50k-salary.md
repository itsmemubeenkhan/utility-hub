A $50,000 salary can buy a home, but the margin for error is thin, so the math has to be exact. If you are asking **what mortgage can i afford on 50k salary**, the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule** puts the answer at roughly a **$145,000 to $185,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, depending on whether taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget (they should be). Asked another way, what mortgage can i afford with 50k salary comes down to one monthly number: **$1,166.67** for all housing costs. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs at or below 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts at or below 36% of gross monthly income

On $50,000 a year ($4,166.67 a month):

- Front-end limit: $4,166.67 x 0.28 = **$1,166.67 a month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $4,166.67 x 0.36 = **$1,500 a month** for everything

With no other debts, the front-end number binds: $1,166.67 is your total housing budget, including principal, interest, taxes, insurance and PMI. ## From $1,166.67 a month to a loan amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $1,166.67 a month in principal and interest alone supports a loan of about **$185,000**. But that $1,166.67 must also cover property taxes and insurance, which lenders count as housing. After roughly $250 a month for taxes and insurance, the principal-and-interest budget is closer to $916.67, supporting a loan of about **$145,000**. That gap is why two calculators can give you two different answers on the same salary: one ignores taxes and insurance, the other does not. Trust the lower one.

With 20% down, the $145,000 loan buys roughly a **$181,000 home**. With 10% down it buys roughly a $161,000 home, and with an FHA 3.5% down payment roughly a $150,000 home, before PMI.

## What a $200,000 home actually costs a month

Here is the uncomfortable truth at this income: a $200,000 home does not fit the guideline at 6.5%, even with 20% down. The same house costs very different amounts depending on your down payment, because PMI and the loan size both move:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($7,000) | $193,000 | $1,219.89 | $250 | $88.46 | $1,558.35 |
| 10% ($20,000) | $180,000 | $1,137.72 | $250 | $75.00 | $1,462.72 |
| 20% ($40,000) | $160,000 | $1,011.31 | $250 | $0 | $1,261.31 |

Every version is over the $1,166.67 guideline, by $95 to $392 a month. The home that does fit with 20% down is closer to **$180,000**: a $144,000 loan costs about $910.18 in principal and interest, plus $250 in taxes and insurance, for **$1,160.18 a month**, just inside the budget. ## Debts shrink the number fast

The back-end ratio is where real buyers lose borrowing power. With debts, the lender uses whichever limit is lower:

| Monthly debts | Back-end housing cap (36% minus debts) | Loan it supports at 6.5%, 30 years, after ~$250 tax and insurance |
|---|---|---|
| $0 | $1,500 (front-end $1,166.67 binds) | ~$145,000 |
| $400 (car payment) | $1,100 | ~$134,000 |
| $700 (car + student loans) | $800 | ~$87,000 |

On an apples-to-apples basis, $400 a month in debts cuts roughly $11,000 off your loan versus no debts, and $700 a month cuts roughly $58,000. Paying off a car before you buy is often worth more than a year of extra saving. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator).

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

Most $50K earners are paid every two weeks: $50,000 / 26 = **$1,923.08 per paycheck** (gross), so the 28% housing budget is **$538.46 per paycheck**. Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. On a $150,000 loan at 6.5%, adding $100 a month pays it off in 277 months instead of 360 and saves about **$51,724 in interest**.

## Should you borrow the max?

$1,166.67 a month is the ceiling, not the target. On $50K gross, take-home is roughly $3,500 a month after federal tax and FICA, before state tax, so a maxed-out payment leaves about $2,300 for everything else. Buying 10 to 15% under your max is the sweet spot between building equity and sleeping well. ## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $50K a year?
Roughly a **$145,000 to $185,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $1,166.67 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### How much mortgage payment can I afford on $50K salary?
Up to **$1,166.67 a month** for all housing costs. A $180,000 home with 20% down costs about $1,160 a month, which fits; a $200,000 home with 20% down costs about $1,261, which does not.

### Can I afford a $250K house on a $50K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $250,000 home costs about $1,514 a month including taxes and insurance, well over the $1,166.67 guideline. You would need roughly a **$65,000 salary** to fit it comfortably at 6.5%.

### How much mortgage can I afford on $50K salary with debts?
With $400 a month in debts, the cap is $1,100 a month and your realistic loan falls to roughly $134,000 after taxes and insurance. With $700 a month in debts, the cap is $800 and the loan falls to roughly $87,000.

### What is a good down payment on a $50K salary?
20% ($36,000 to $40,000 on a $180,000 to $200,000 home) avoids PMI entirely, but it is a big sum at this income, so 10% with PMI of roughly $75 a month is a realistic middle path. Saving the 20% while paying rent takes most buyers 3 to 5 years.

### Does a $50K salary guarantee mortgage approval?
No. The 28/36 math sets the ceiling, but approval also depends on credit score, employment history, down payment source and the appraisal.
