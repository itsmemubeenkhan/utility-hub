$150,000 is nearly double the US median household income — you can afford a genuinely nice home in almost any market, with room to spare. Using the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule**, the answer lands around **$520k–$575k** depending on your debts, down payment and rate. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income

On $150,000/year ($12,500/month):

- Front-end limit: $12,500 × 0.28 = **$3,500/month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $12,500 × 0.36 = $4,500/month for everything

If you have $900/month in car and student loan payments, the back-end rule allows $4,500 − $900 = $3,600 for housing, so the front-end $3,500 is the binding limit.

## Converting the payment to a price

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $3,500/month in principal and interest supports a loan of about **$554,000**. With 20% down, that means a home price of roughly **$692,000**, but wait. That $3,500 must also cover property taxes and insurance (PITI), which lenders include in "housing." After ~$600/month for taxes and insurance, the P&I budget is closer to $2,902, supporting a loan of ~$459,000 and a price of **~$575,000** with 20% down.

Realistic ranges on $150K salary:

| Down payment | Approx. max home price (6.5%, 30-yr) |
|---|---|
| 10% down | ~$520,000 |
| 20% down | ~$575,000 |
| Debts over $1,400/mo | subtract ~$65,000 |

## Five things that move the number

1. **Interest rate.** At 5.5% instead of 6.5%, the same payment buys ~12% more house. Rate matters more than most buyers think.
2. **Existing debts.** Every $200/month in debt payments cuts roughly $30,000 off your max price. Killing a car payment before buying is powerful.
3. **Down payment.** On $150K, consider going beyond 20% down: every extra $50,000 down cuts roughly $316/month off the payment at 6.5%, and a bigger cushion protects you if prices dip.
4. **Property taxes.** In New Jersey, taxes on a home in this range can exceed $1,100/month; in Texas, ~$800; in Washington state, under $500. Location changes affordability enormously.
5. **HOA dues.** A $300/month HOA fee counts against your ratios exactly like a higher mortgage payment.

## The costs buyers forget

The mortgage is only the headline. Budget ~1% of the home's value per year for maintenance ($5,800/year on a $575,000 home), plus utilities, and an emergency fund that covers 3–6 months of the *new* higher housing cost, not your old rent. Higher price means higher stakes on the inspection and appraisal — and budget 1% a year for maintenance on a bigger, more complex home.

## Should you max it out?

The 28/36 rule is a **ceiling**, not a target. On $150K, the risk isn't qualifying — it's lifestyle inflation. Buying 10–20% below your max and investing the difference builds wealth far faster than a bigger house does.
