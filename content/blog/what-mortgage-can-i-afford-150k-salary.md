If **you make $150K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$475,000-$554,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$525,000-$595,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 150k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $475,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $554,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$150,000 ÷ 12 = **$12,500/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $12,500 × 0.28 = **$3,500/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $12,500 × 0.36 = **$4,500/month**

That $3,500 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $3,500/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $3,500 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$554,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $3,500 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $3,000 → **≈ $475,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$475K-$554K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## Can you afford a $600,000 house on a $150K salary?

Almost exactly. A $600,000 home at 6.5% on a 30-year loan sits right on the $3,500 monthly housing budget at 20% down, and misses it at lower down payments:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month | vs budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($21,000) | $579,000 | $3,659.67 | $500 | $241.25 | $4,400.92 | $900.92 over |

| 20% ($120,000) | $480,000 | $3,033.93 | $500 | $0 | $3,533.93 | $33.93 over |

With 20% down, the $600,000 home runs just $33.93 a month over the guideline - close enough that a slightly lower rate or a $5,000 bigger down payment closes the gap. With 10% down it runs $638.17 over the guideline, mostly because of PMI. If you are short on down payment, read [what is PMI and how to avoid it](/blog/what-is-pmi-how-to-avoid) before you decide. To afford a $650,000 home comfortably at 6.5% with 20% down, you would need roughly a **$162,000 salary**: a $520,000 loan costs $3,286.75 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $3,786.75 a month, which needs $13,524 a month gross at the 28% rule.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $4,500/month:

- **No debts:** full $3,500 housing budget → ~$475K-$554K mortgage
- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $3,500, exactly your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$475,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $3,000 → mortgage drops to roughly **$396,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $2,500 → mortgage roughly **$316,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 150k salary with debts** precisely, here are three debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $3,500 | ~$475,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $3,000 | ~$396,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $2,500 | ~$316,000 |

At $2,000 a month in debts your borrowing power is roughly two-thirds of what it is with no debts. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator) before you start house-hunting.

## What about a 15-year mortgage?

On $150K, a 15-year loan is tight but not impossible. At 6.0%, $475,000 over 15 years costs **$4,008.32/month** in P&I alone - already over your $3,500 housing budget before taxes and insurance. A smaller 15-year loan of about $415,000 fits ($3,500 P&I at 6.0%), but most buyers are better off with the 30-year term and extra principal payments when they can.

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $150K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $150,000 ÷ 26 = **$5,769.23**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $3,500 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$1,615.38**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $475,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$1,385.69** (the rest of the $1,615.38 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $150K salary?
Roughly a **$475,000 to $554,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $3,500 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford a $600K house on a $150K salary?
Just about. With 20% down, a $600,000 home costs about **$3,533.93 a month** including taxes and insurance, $33.93 over the $3,500 budget. With 10% down it costs about $4,138.17 a month, $638.17 over the guideline.

### Can I afford a $650K house on a $150K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $650,000 home costs about **$3,786.75 a month** including taxes and insurance, $286.75 over the $3,500 budget. You would need roughly a **$162,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### What mortgage can I afford on $150K salary with debts?
With $1,000 a month in debts, about **$475,000** (the back-end cap equals the front-end budget). With $1,500 a month in debts, about **$396,000**. With $2,000 a month in debts, about **$316,000**. All figures are after taxes and insurance at 6.5% on a 30-year loan.

### How much house can I afford on $150K with 20% down?
About a **$594,000 home**: a $475,000 loan costs $3,002.32 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $3,502.32 a month, right at the 28% guideline. A $600,000 home with 20% down is $33.93 a month over.

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $150,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$3,500/month** for housing, supporting about a **$475,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $554,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $150K, your housing budget is **$1,615.38 per paycheck** ($5,769.23 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $475,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $1,385.69 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
