If **you make $140K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$438,000-$517,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$485,000-$550,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 140k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $438,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $517,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$140,000 ÷ 12 = **$11,666.67/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $11,666.67 × 0.28 = **$3,266.67/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $11,666.67 × 0.36 = **$4,200/month**

That $3,266.67 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $3,266.67/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $3,266.67 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$517,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $3,266.67 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $2,766.67 → **≈ $438,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$438K-$517K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## Can you afford a $550,000 house on a $140K salary?

Almost exactly. A $550,000 home at 6.5% on a 30-year loan sits right on the $3,266.67 monthly housing budget at 20% down, and misses it at lower down payments:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month | vs budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($19,250) | $530,750 | $3,354.70 | $500 | $221.15 | $4,075.85 | $809.18 over |

| 20% ($110,000) | $440,000 | $2,781.10 | $500 | $0 | $3,281.10 | $14.43 over |

With 20% down, the $550,000 home runs just $14.43 a month over the guideline - close enough that a slightly lower rate or a $5,000 bigger down payment closes the gap. With 10% down it runs $547.70 over the guideline, mostly because of PMI. If you are short on down payment, read [what is PMI and how to avoid it](/blog/what-is-pmi-how-to-avoid) before you decide. To afford a $600,000 home comfortably at 6.5% with 20% down, you would need roughly a **$151,000 salary**: a $480,000 loan costs $3,033.93 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $3,533.93 a month, which needs $12,621 a month gross at the 28% rule.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $4,200/month:

- **No debts:** full $3,266.67 housing budget → ~$438K-$517K mortgage
- **$1,000/month** (car payment): housing capped at $3,200 → mortgage drops to roughly **$427,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $2,700 → mortgage drops to roughly **$348,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $2,200 → mortgage roughly **$269,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 140k salary with debts** precisely, here are three debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $3,200 | ~$427,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $2,700 | ~$348,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $2,200 | ~$269,000 |

At $2,000 a month in debts your borrowing power is roughly 60% of what it is with no debts. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator) before you start house-hunting.

## What about a 15-year mortgage?

On $140K, a 15-year loan is tight but not impossible. At 6.0%, $438,000 over 15 years costs **$3,696.09/month** in P&I alone - already over your $3,266.67 housing budget before taxes and insurance. A smaller 15-year loan of about $387,000 fits ($3,266.67 P&I at 6.0%), but most buyers are better off with the 30-year term and extra principal payments when they can.

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $140K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $140,000 ÷ 26 = **$5,384.62**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $3,266.67 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$1,507.69**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $438,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$1,276.92** (the rest of the $1,507.69 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $140K salary?
Roughly a **$438,000 to $517,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $3,266.67 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford a $550K house on a $140K salary?
Just about. With 20% down, a $550,000 home costs about **$3,281.10 a month** including taxes and insurance, $14.43 over the $3,266.67 budget. With 10% down it costs about $3,814.37 a month, $547.70 over the guideline.

### Can I afford a $600K house on a $140K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $600,000 home costs about **$3,533.93 a month** including taxes and insurance, $267.26 over the $3,266.67 budget. You would need roughly a **$151,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### What mortgage can I afford on $140K salary with debts?
With $1,000 a month in debts, about **$427,000**. With $1,500 a month in debts, about **$348,000**. With $2,000 a month in debts, about **$269,000**. All figures are after taxes and insurance at 6.5% on a 30-year loan.

### How much house can I afford on $140K with 20% down?
About a **$548,000 home**: a $438,000 loan costs $2,768.46 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $3,268.46 a month, right at the 28% guideline. A $550,000 home with 20% down is $14.43 a month over.

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $140,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$3,266.67/month** for housing, supporting about a **$438,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $517,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $140K, your housing budget is **$1,507.69 per paycheck** ($5,384.62 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $438,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $1,276.92 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
