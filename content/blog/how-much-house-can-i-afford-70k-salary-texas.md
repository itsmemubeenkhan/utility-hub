$70,000 in Texas stretches further than in most states - **no state income tax** means your take-home pay is higher, and Texas home prices remain reasonable outside Austin.

## Texas buying power on $70K

Using the 28/36 rule with Texas averages:

- **Gross monthly:** $5,833
- **Front-end limit (28%):** $1,633/month for housing
- **Texas property tax:** ~1.6% (higher than national average - this matters!)
- **Homeowner insurance:** ~$1,800/year in Texas

With 10% down and 6.5% rate, that $1,633/month buys roughly **$245k–$280k** depending on the county's tax rate.

## Why Texas is different

1. **No state income tax** - you keep ~$3,000+/year vs. living in California
2. **Higher property taxes** - Texas offsets no income tax with 1.6-2.2% property tax
3. **Insurance costs** - hail and hurricane zones raise premiums

## Best Texas metros for $70K buyers

- **San Antonio:** Median ~$280k - within reach
- **Houston:** Median ~$310k - stretch, but possible in suburbs
- **Dallas-Fort Worth:** Median ~$350k - tough, look at outer suburbs
- **Austin:** Median ~$450k - out of range on $70K alone
- **El Paso:** Median ~$220k - comfortable

## The math

On $70,000/year ($5,833/month):

- Max housing payment: $1,633/month
- Minus property tax (~$330 on $250k home)
- Minus insurance (~$150)
- Minus PMI if <20% down (~$120)
- **Available for P&I:** ~$1,033/month
- **Home price at 6.5%, 30yr:** ~$245,000
