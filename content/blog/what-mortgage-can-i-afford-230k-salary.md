If **you make $230K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$770,000-$849,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$856,000-$963,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 230k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $770,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $849,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$230,000 ÷ 12 = **$19,166.67/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $19,166.67 × 0.28 = **$5,366.67/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $19,166.67 × 0.36 = **$6,900/month**

That $5,366.67 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $5,366.67/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $5,366.67 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$849,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $5,366.67 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $4,866.67 → **≈ $770,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$770K-$849K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $6,900/month, but on $230K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $1,533 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $5,900, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$770,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $5,400, still just above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$770,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $4,900 → mortgage drops to roughly **$696,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $4,400 → mortgage roughly **$617,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $3,900 → mortgage roughly **$538,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 230k salary with debts** precisely, here are five debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $5,366.67 | ~$770,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $5,366.67 | ~$770,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $4,900 | ~$696,000 |
| $2,500 (car + loans + cards) | $4,400 | ~$617,000 |
| $3,000 (car + loans + cards) | $3,900 | ~$538,000 |

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $230K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $230,000 ÷ 26 = **$8,846.15**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $5,366.67 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$2,476.92**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $770,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$2,246.27** (the rest of the $2,476.92 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $230K salary?
Roughly a **$770,000 to $849,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $5,366.67 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford a $950K house on a $230K salary?
Yes, with 20% down. A $950,000 home costs about **$5,303.72 a month** including taxes and insurance, $62.95 under the $5,366.67 budget. With 10% down it costs about $6,296.06 a month, $929.39 over the guideline, so 20% down is the sensible route at this price.

### Can I afford a $900K house on a $230K salary?
Yes. With 20% down, a $900,000 home costs about **$5,050.89 a month** including taxes and insurance, $315.78 under the $5,366.67 budget. With 10% down it costs about $5,991.00 a month, $624.33 over the guideline.

### Can I afford a $1M house on a $230K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $1,000,000 home costs about **$5,556.54 a month** including taxes and insurance, $189.88 over the $5,366.67 budget. You would need roughly a **$238,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### What mortgage can I afford on $230K salary with debts?
With $1,000 or $1,500 a month in debts, about **$770,000** (the back-end cap stays above the front-end budget). With $2,000 a month in debts, about **$696,000**. With $2,500 a month in debts, about **$617,000**. With $3,000 a month in debts, about **$538,000**. All figures are after taxes and insurance at 6.5% on a 30-year loan.

### How much house can I afford on $230K with 20% down?
About a **$963,000 home**: a $770,000 loan costs $4,866.92 in principal and interest plus $500 in taxes and insurance, or $5,366.92 a month, right at the 28% guideline. A $950,000 home with 20% down is $62.95 a month under.

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $230,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$5,366.67/month** for housing, supporting about a **$770,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $849,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $230K, your housing budget is **$2,476.92 per paycheck** ($8,846.15 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $770,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $2,246.27 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Does a $1,500 monthly car payment reduce what I can borrow on $230K?
No. The back-end cap with $1,500 in debts is $5,400 a month, still above your $5,366.67 front-end budget, so your borrowing power stays at about **$770,000**. Debts only start shrinking it once they pass about $1,533 a month.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
