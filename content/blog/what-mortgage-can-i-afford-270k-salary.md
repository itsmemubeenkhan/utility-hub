If **you make $270K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$918,000-$997,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$1,020,000-$1,246,000** range depending on your down payment. ## Start with your monthly income

$270,000 ÷ 12 = **$22,500.00/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $22,500.00 × 0.28 = **$6,300.00/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $22,500.00 × 0.36 = **$8,100/month**

That $6,300.00 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $6,300.00/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $6,300.00 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$997,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $6,300.00 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $5,800.00 → **≈ $918,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$918K-$997K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $8,100/month, but on $270K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $1,800 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $7,100, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$918,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $6,600, still just above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$918,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,100 → mortgage drops to roughly **$886,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,600 → mortgage roughly **$807,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,100 → mortgage roughly **$728,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 270k salary with debts** precisely, here are five debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $6,300.00 | ~$918,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $6,300.00 | ~$918,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $6,100 | ~$886,000 |
| $2,500 (car + loans + cards) | $5,600 | ~$807,000 |
| $3,000 (car + loans + cards) | $5,100 | ~$728,000 |

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $270K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $270,000 ÷ 26 = **$10,384.62**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $6,300.00 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$2,907.69**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $918,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$2,678.02** (the rest of the $2,907.69 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.
