Paying your mortgage every two weeks instead of once a month sounds like a scheduling preference. It is actually one of the highest-return moves a homeowner can make: on a typical loan it erases years of payments and six figures of interest without increasing your monthly budget.

## How the trick works

A biweekly plan splits your monthly payment in half and charges it every 14 days. There are 26 biweekly periods in a year, so you make **26 half-payments = 13 full monthly payments** instead of 12. That 13th payment goes entirely to principal, and every principal dollar you pay early cancels all the interest it would have earned the bank over the remaining life of the loan.

## The exact numbers

On a $400,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years (monthly payment $2,528):

| | Monthly | Biweekly |
|---|---|---|
| Payment rhythm | $2,528/month | $1,264 every 2 weeks |
| Payoff time | 30.0 years | ~24.2 years |
| Total interest | $510,178 | ~$392,682 |
| **Interest saved** | - | **~$117,496** |
| **Years saved** | - | **~5.8 years** |

Same dollars per month on average. Nearly six years of your life back, and $117,000 that stays yours. Run your own numbers in our [biweekly mortgage calculator](/tools/biweekly-mortgage-calculator).

## The catch: lender programs

Many lenders and third-party companies sell "biweekly payment programs" with setup fees ($300–$500) or per-payment charges. Worse, some hold your half-payments in a suspense account until a full payment accumulates, which delays the principal reduction and shrinks the benefit. Never pay a fee for this.

## The free DIY alternative

You can replicate ~95% of the savings with zero fees: divide your monthly principal-and-interest payment by 12 and add that amount as **extra principal** with each monthly payment. On the $2,528 example, that is an extra $211/month toward principal. Just write "apply to principal" on the payment or set it in your lender portal, and confirm the first statement shows the principal actually dropping faster.

## Who should do this

Biweekly (or DIY extra principal) makes sense if you plan to stay in the home long enough for the compounding to matter, you have no higher-interest debt, and your emergency fund is solid. If you carry credit-card balances at 20%+, kill those first - the return is nearly triple. And if your mortgage rate is under 4%, investing the extra in a retirement account will usually beat prepaying over long horizons.

## Bottom line

Biweekly payments are not magic; they are just a disciplined way to make one extra principal payment per year. Whether through your lender's free program or the DIY extra-principal method, it is the closest thing to free money in home finance.
