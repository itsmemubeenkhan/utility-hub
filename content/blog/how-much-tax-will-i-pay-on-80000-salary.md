If you earn **$80,000 a year**, you will pay roughly **$14,890** in federal income tax plus FICA in 2026 as a single filer - leaving you with **$65,110 take-home**, or about **$5,426 per month**. Here is the exact bracket-by-bracket math, so you can see where every dollar goes.

## The short answer

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross salary | $80,000 |
| Federal income tax | $8,770 |
| Social Security + Medicare (FICA) | $6,120 |
| **Total tax** | **$14,890** |
| **Take-home pay** | **$65,110** |
| Monthly take-home | $5,426 |
| Effective tax rate | 18.6% |

These numbers assume you are a **single filer** taking the **2026 standard deduction of $16,100**, with no state income tax. Your actual take-home will be lower if your state has an income tax.

## Federal income tax: $8,770

First, subtract the standard deduction:

- $80,000 − $16,100 = **$63,900 taxable income**

Then apply the 2026 single-filer brackets:

| Bracket | Taxable slice | Rate | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0 – $12,400 | $12,400 | 10% | $1,240 |
| $12,401 – $50,400 | $38,000 | 12% | $4,560 |
| $50,401 – $63,900 | $13,500 | 22% | $2,970 |
| **Total** | | | **$8,770** |

Notice that only the dollars *above* $50,400 are taxed at 22%. Your **marginal rate** is 22%, but your **effective federal rate** is just 11.0% ($8,770 ÷ $80,000). This is the most misunderstood part of the tax code - moving into a higher bracket never taxes your whole income at that rate.

## FICA: $6,120

FICA is flat and unavoidable on wages:

- **Social Security:** 6.2% × $80,000 = $4,960 (the 2026 wage base is $184,500, so all of your salary is subject to it)
- **Medicare:** 1.45% × $80,000 = $1,160
- **Total FICA:** $6,120

Your employer matches this amount behind the scenes, but as a W-2 employee you never see that half.

## What changes the number

1. **State income tax.** In California (~9.3% effective at this income) you would lose another ~$4,500; in Texas or Florida, $0. State tax is the single biggest variable.
2. **Pre-tax deductions.** 401(k) contributions, HSA contributions, and health insurance premiums reduce your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. Maxing a 401(k) at $23,500 would cut your federal tax by roughly $5,170.
3. **Filing status.** Married filing jointly roughly doubles the brackets and the standard deduction ($32,200), which can significantly lower the bill for single-earner households.
4. **Freelance vs employee.** If this $80,000 is 1099 income instead of W-2, you owe self-employment tax (15.3%) instead of half of FICA - but you can deduct half of it. ## FAQ

### How much tax will I pay on an $80,000 salary in 2026?
As a single filer taking the standard deduction, about **$14,890** total - $8,770 in federal income tax plus $6,120 in Social Security and Medicare. Your take-home is roughly **$65,110 per year** ($5,426/month) before state taxes.

### What is the take-home pay on $80K salary?
Approximately **$65,110 per year** after federal income tax and FICA, or **$5,426 per month** / **$2,504 biweekly**. State income tax and pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance) will change this.

### What tax bracket is $80,000 salary in?
Your **marginal** federal bracket is **22%**, but that rate applies only to income above $50,400 (single filer, 2026). Your **effective** federal rate is about 11%.

### How much Social Security and Medicare tax on $80K?
**$6,120** total - $4,960 for Social Security (6.2%) and $1,160 for Medicare (1.45%). The 2026 Social Security wage base is $184,500, so your full salary is subject to it.

### Is $80K salary taxed more than $70K?
Yes, but only the extra $10,000 is taxed at your 22% marginal rate (plus 7.65% FICA). You keep roughly $7,035 of the extra $10,000. You are never worse off earning more under a progressive tax system.
