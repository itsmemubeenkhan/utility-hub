A **650 credit score is enough to buy a house** in 2026 — but it sits in the "fair" range, which means you will pay more in interest than someone with a 750. For the full picture on mortgage score tiers, see [what counts as a good credit score for a mortgage](/blog/good-credit-score-for-mortgage). Here is exactly what a 650 gets you, what it costs, and how to push it higher before you apply.

## The short answer

| Loan type | 650 score? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| FHA loan | **Yes** | 580+ qualifies for 3.5% down; 650 is comfortable |
| Conventional | **Yes, but** | 620 minimum met; expect a rate ~0.5–0.75% above top-tier |
| VA loan | **Yes** | No official minimum; most lenders want 620+ |
| USDA loan | **Yes** | 640 minimum at most lenders |

You will not be denied for the score alone. The real question is **price**.

## What 650 costs you vs 750

On a $350,000 home with 10% down ($315,000 loan, 30-year fixed):

| Credit score | Approx. rate (2026) | Monthly P&I | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 750+ | 6.25% | $1,939 | ~$383,000 |
| 700 | 6.50% | $1,991 | ~$402,000 |
| **650** | **6.875%** | **$2,070** | **~$430,000** |
| 620 | 7.25% | $2,149 | ~$459,000 |

The gap between 650 and 750 is roughly **$131/month** — about **$47,000 in extra interest** over 30 years. That is the true cost of a "fair" score. (Rates are illustrative; check current rates when you apply.)

Conventional loans also add **PMI** (private mortgage insurance) when you put down less than 20%, and PMI is *more expensive* at lower scores — another $50–$150/month at 650 vs 750.

## Why FHA is often better at 650

FHA loans are built for scores like yours:

- **3.5% down** with 580+ (vs 3–5% conventional, harder to get approved at 650)
- **More forgiving** debt-to-income limits (up to 56.9% with compensating factors)
- **Rate barely depends on score** — a 650 and a 750 get nearly the same FHA rate

The tradeoff: FHA charges **upfront MIP** (1.75% of the loan, rolled in) plus **annual MIP** (~0.55%/year) for at least 11 years — and for the life of the loan if you put down less than 10%. Run both scenarios before deciding — our [FHA vs conventional loan guide](/blog/fha-vs-conventional-loan) breaks down the full cost comparison.

## 5 fastest ways to lift 650 toward 700+

1. **Pay down revolving balances.** Utilization (balance ÷ limit) is ~30% of your score. Getting every card under 30% — ideally under 10% — can add 20–60 points in one billing cycle.
2. **Do not close old cards.** Length of history matters; closing your oldest card shortens your average age of credit.
3. **Dispute errors.** About 1 in 5 reports has an error. One removed collection can jump you 30+ points.
4. **Become an authorized user.** A family member's old, clean card can import years of good history.
5. **No new applications** in the 3–6 months before house hunting — each hard inquiry dings you a few points.

Even 3 months of focused work can move 650 → 690, which meaningfully improves conventional pricing.

## Should you buy now or wait?

**Buy now with 650 if:** you found the right home, you have the down payment plus a 3–6 month emergency fund, and the monthly payment fits comfortably at today's rates. You can refinance later if your score improves.

**Wait and improve if:** you are borderline on approval, the payment would stretch you thin, or you can realistically hit 700+ within 6 months — the lifetime savings are enormous.

## FAQ

### Can I buy a house with a 650 credit score?
**Yes.** FHA loans approve 650 easily with 3.5% down; conventional loans (620 minimum) and VA/USDA loans are also options. You will pay a higher interest rate than borrowers with 700+ scores.

### What mortgage rate will I get with 650 credit score?
Roughly **0.5–0.75% above** the best advertised rates — around 6.875% when top-tier borrowers get 6.25% (illustrative 2026 figures). FHA rates vary less by score.

### Is 650 a good credit score for FHA?
**Yes** — 650 is comfortably above FHA's 580 threshold for 3.5% down. FHA is often the best loan type at this score because its rates barely depend on credit score.

### How much house can I afford with a 650 credit score?
The score does not set your price — your **income, debts, and down payment** do (see the 28/36 rule). But the higher rate at 650 means the same income buys slightly less house than at 750. Check our [home affordability calculator](/tools/home-affordability-calculator).

### How fast can I raise my score from 650 to 700?
**1–6 months** with focused work: pay revolving balances under 10% utilization, dispute errors, avoid new applications. Utilization changes reflect in as little as one billing cycle.
