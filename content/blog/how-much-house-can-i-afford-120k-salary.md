$120,000 is roughly 50% above the US median household income — firmly upper-middle class in most of the country, and enough for a comfortable home nearly anywhere outside the priciest metros. Using the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule**, the answer lands around **$415k–$460k** depending on your debts, down payment and rate. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income

On $120,000/year ($10,000/month):

- Front-end limit: $10,000 × 0.28 = **$2,800/month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $10,000 × 0.36 = $3,600/month for everything

If you have $700/month in car and student loan payments, the back-end rule allows $3,600 − $700 = $2,900 for housing, so the front-end $2,800 is the binding limit.

## Converting the payment to a price

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $2,800/month in principal and interest supports a loan of about **$443,000**. With 20% down, that means a home price of roughly **$554,000**, but wait. That $2,800 must also cover property taxes and insurance (PITI), which lenders include in "housing." After ~$480/month for taxes and insurance, the P&I budget is closer to $2,322, supporting a loan of ~$367,000 and a price of **~$460,000** with 20% down.

Realistic ranges on $120K salary:

| Down payment | Approx. max home price (6.5%, 30-yr) |
|---|---|
| 10% down | ~$415,000 |
| 20% down | ~$460,000 |
| Debts over $1,200/mo | subtract ~$65,000 |

## Five things that move the number

1. **Interest rate.** At 5.5% instead of 6.5%, the same payment buys ~12% more house. Rate matters more than most buyers think.
2. **Existing debts.** Every $200/month in debt payments cuts roughly $30,000 off your max price. Killing a car payment before buying is powerful.
3. **Down payment.** On $120K, 20% down is very achievable, and putting down more starts to make real sense — every extra $50,000 down cuts roughly $316/month off the payment at 6.5%.
4. **Property taxes.** In New Jersey, taxes on a home in this range can top $1,000/month; in Texas, ~$700; in Colorado, under $300. Location changes affordability enormously.
5. **HOA dues.** A $300/month HOA fee counts against your ratios exactly like a higher mortgage payment.

## The costs buyers forget

The mortgage is only the headline. Budget ~1% of the home's value per year for maintenance ($4,600/year on a $460,000 home), plus utilities, and an emergency fund that covers 3–6 months of the *new* higher housing cost, not your old rent. Higher price means higher stakes on the inspection and appraisal — a $1,000 inspection on a $450K purchase is rounding error.

## Should you max it out?

The 28/36 rule is a **ceiling**, not a target. On $120K it's tempting to stretch — but buying 10–20% below your max leaves room for investing, travel and life. Being "house poor" at a high income is just lifestyle inflation with a mortgage.
