## What a pay stub generator actually does

A paystub generator takes your employer and employee details, pay period, hours or salary, and deductions, then formats them as a standard pay stub: gross pay, each deduction, net pay, and year-to-date totals. It does not verify your income and it does not pay you. Its only job is arithmetic and formatting, which is why the numbers you type in matter so much. ## Worked example: $25 an hour, paid biweekly

Take an hourly worker earning $25 an hour on a biweekly schedule (80 hours per pay period):

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross pay | $25 x 80 hours | $2,000.00 |
| Social Security (6.2%) | $2,000 x 0.062 | $124.00 |
| Medicare (1.45%) | $2,000 x 0.0145 | $29.00 |
| Total FICA (7.65%) | $124 + $29 | $153.00 |
| Federal withholding (example) | varies by W-4 | $150.00 |
| State tax (5% example) | $2,000 x 0.05 | $100.00 |
| **Net pay** | $2,000 - $153 - $150 - $100 | **$1,597.00** |

FICA is the only part that is fixed by law: 6.2% for Social Security plus 1.45% for Medicare, 7.65% combined, on every paycheck. Federal withholding depends on your W-4 and filing status, and state tax depends on where you live, so those two lines are examples, not quotes. If your stub shows FICA as anything other than 7.65% of gross (before the Social Security wage cap), the stub is wrong.

## What every pay stub must show

A landlord or lender will reject a stub that is missing pieces. Yours needs all of these:

- **Employer name and address**, and employee name and address
- **Pay period dates** (not just the pay date) and the pay date itself
- **Pay rate and hours**, or salary basis, so gross pay can be rechecked
- **Gross pay, every deduction itemized, and net pay**
- **Year-to-date totals** for gross pay, deductions, and net pay
- **Pay frequency**: weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly

Biweekly and semimonthly are not the same. Biweekly means 26 paychecks a year; semimonthly means 24. On a $60,000 salary, biweekly gross is $60,000 / 26 = **$2,307.69** per paycheck, while semimonthly gross is $60,000 / 24 = **$2,500.00**. Mixing them up is the most common stub error, and it changes every year-to-date figure downstream.

## Year-to-date totals, done right

Year-to-date (YTD) figures are just per-paycheck figures multiplied by the number of pay periods so far. For the $25-an-hour worker above ($2,000 gross, $153 FICA per period):

| Pay periods so far | YTD gross | YTD FICA |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $2,000 | $153 |
| 5 | $10,000 | $765 |
| 13 | $26,000 | $1,989 |
| 26 (full year) | $52,000 | $3,978 |

If your stub's YTD gross does not equal gross-per-period times periods worked, anyone checking will spot it instantly. The same test works for the $60,000 salary example: after 10 biweekly periods, YTD gross is **$23,076.92** and YTD FICA is **$1,765.38**, exactly 10 times the per-period figures of $2,307.69 and $176.54.

## Overtime changes the gross, not the formula

At $20 an hour with 5 overtime hours in a week, overtime pays time-and-a-half ($30 an hour): 40 regular hours give $800, plus 5 overtime hours give $150, for a **$950** weekly gross. Over a biweekly period with two such weeks, gross is **$1,900**, and FICA on that period is $1,900 x 0.0765 = **$145.35**. The stub must show regular and overtime hours on separate lines, or the gross cannot be verified.

## If you are self-employed

Self-employed workers do not get employer pay stubs, which is exactly why a paystub generator is useful for them: you document your own draws. Base the stub on real business income, not a round number you wish you earned, and keep the invoices or bank deposits behind it. A stub that claims $2,000 biweekly while your bank shows $1,200 will fail any verification. ## The fake-stub warning, plainly

Never use a paystub generator to create income you did not earn for a loan, lease, or benefits application. That is fraud, and verification services, employer calls, and bank statements catch it routinely. The penalty is far worse than a rejected application. A generator is a formatting tool for real pay, nothing more.

## FAQ

### What is a paycheck stub generator?
A tool that turns your pay details (employer, pay period, hours or salary, deductions) into a formatted pay stub showing gross pay, itemized deductions, net pay, and year-to-date totals. It calculates and formats; it does not verify income or create pay you did not earn.

### Is a free paystub generator legitimate?
Yes, when it records pay you actually earned. Employers, freelancers, and self-employed workers use them for real documentation. It is illegal to use one to fabricate income for a loan, rental, or benefits application.

### What information must be on a pay stub?
Employer and employee names and addresses, pay period dates, pay date, pay rate and hours (or salary basis), gross pay, each deduction itemized, net pay, year-to-date totals, and pay frequency. Missing any of these makes the stub hard to verify.

### How do I calculate net pay on a pay stub?
Start with gross pay, subtract FICA (7.65%: 6.2% Social Security plus 1.45% Medicare), then subtract federal withholding, state tax, and any other deductions. Example: $2,000 gross minus $153 FICA, $150 federal, and $100 state tax leaves **$1,597** net.

### Is a check stub the same as a pay stub?
Yes. Check stub, pay stub, paycheck stub, and paystub all mean the same document: the earnings statement that accompanies (or replaces) a paycheck. A check stub generator and a paystub generator are the same kind of tool.

### How do I make a pay stub if I am self-employed?
Document your actual business income as regular draws, with consistent pay periods and year-to-date totals, and keep invoices or bank records behind the figures. Base every number on money you really received; a stub that does not match your bank deposits will fail verification.
