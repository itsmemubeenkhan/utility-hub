Closing costs are the fees and prepayments due on the day you sign, separate from your down payment. On a typical purchase they run **2% to 5% of the home price**: $6,000 to $15,000 on a $300,000 house. This guide breaks a realistic $300,000 closing down line by line so you can see exactly where the money goes.

## The example closing: $300,000 house, 20% down

Our buyer puts $60,000 down and borrows **$240,000 at 6.5%**. Closing costs fall into three buckets: lender and third-party fees, title and government charges, and prepaids (money collected upfront for future bills).

### Bucket 1: Lender and third-party fees

| Fee | How it is set | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Loan origination | 0.5% of the loan | $1,200 |
| Appraisal | Flat, local market | $400 |
| Credit report | Flat | $60 |

### Bucket 2: Title and government

| Fee | What it covers | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Title search | Ownership history check | $350 |
| Lender's title insurance | Protects the lender | $750 |
| Owner's title insurance | Protects you (optional but common) | $1,200 |
| Attorney / settlement | Closing agent fee | $900 |
| Recording fees | County deed and mortgage filing | $125 |

### Bucket 3: Prepaids and escrow deposits

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Home insurance, 12 months prepaid | Full first year upfront | $1,800 |
| Property tax escrow | $275/month tax × 3 months | $825 |
| Insurance escrow | $150/month × 2 months | $300 |
| Prepaid interest | $42.74/day × 15 days to month end | $641.10 |

Prepaid interest deserves a note: interest accrues daily from closing to your first payment, at $240,000 × 6.5% ÷ 365 = **$42.74 a day**. Close on the 16th and about 15 days are collected; close on the 29th and it is a day or two. This is the one closing cost you can shrink just by picking a closing date late in the month.

## The total

| Bucket | Total |
|---|---|
| Lender and third-party fees | $1,660 |
| Title and government | $3,325 |
| Prepaids and escrows | $3,566.10 |
| **All closing costs** | **$8,551.10** |

That is **2.85% of the $300,000 price**, comfortably inside the 2% to 5% range. But notice the cash picture: $60,000 down payment + $8,551.10 closing = **$68,551.10 needed at the table**, before moving costs or any cash reserves the lender wants to see. Buyers who budget only the down payment get an unpleasant surprise a week before closing. ## Which costs are negotiable?

- **Origination and lender fees:** the most negotiable bucket. A competing lender quote is the strongest lever; even 0.25% of the loan is $600 on $240,000.
- **Title insurance and settlement:** in many states you can shop the title company; rates are filed or regulated in others.
- **Appraisal, credit report, recording:** effectively fixed. Recording fees are set by the county.
- **Prepaids:** not fees at all, they are your own future tax and insurance bills collected early. They vary with closing date and local tax rates, not with negotiation.

Seller concessions and lender credits can offset costs too: a lender credit raises your rate slightly in exchange for covering fees, which can make sense if you expect to move or refinance within a few years. Compare the trade-off against a rate buydown using the math in [should I refinance my mortgage](/blog/should-i-refinance-my-mortgage).

## Your Loan Estimate is the early warning

By law you receive a **Loan Estimate** within three business days of applying, with closing costs itemized in the same buckets used here, and a final **Closing Disclosure** at least three business days before signing. Compare the two documents line by line: some fees (recording, and charges you could not shop for) cannot legally grow beyond small tolerances, while escrow deposits can legitimately change. ## FAQ

### How much are closing costs on a $300,000 house?
Typically 2% to 5% of the price, or $6,000 to $15,000. Our worked example totals $8,551.10 (2.85%): $1,660 of lender fees, $3,325 of title and government charges, and $3,566.10 of prepaids and escrow deposits.

### Are closing costs included in the mortgage payment?
No. Closing costs are paid once, at closing, on top of the down payment. Some can be rolled into the loan balance or traded for a slightly higher rate as a lender credit, but the standard case is cash at the table: $68,551.10 total in our $300,000 example with 20% down.

### What is the difference between closing costs and prepaids?
Closing costs are fees for services: origination, appraisal, title, recording. Prepaids are your own upcoming bills (a year of insurance, property tax escrow, and interest to your first payment) collected upfront. In the example, true fees are $4,985 while $3,566.10 is prepaids.

### Can I reduce my closing costs?
Yes, mainly in the lender bucket: get competing quotes, ask for origination discounts, and shop the title company where your state allows it. Choosing a closing date late in the month also cuts prepaid interest, which runs $42.74 a day on a $240,000 loan at 6.5%.

### Who pays closing costs, buyer or seller?
The buyer pays most of them by default, but it is negotiable. Sellers often agree to a concession (a credit toward your costs) in a balanced market, and builders frequently offer closing-cost incentives on new construction.

### When do I find out my exact closing costs?
An itemized Loan Estimate arrives within three business days of your application, and the binding Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Compare them: many fees are legally limited in how much they can increase between the two documents.
