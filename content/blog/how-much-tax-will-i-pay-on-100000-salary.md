If you earn **$100,000 a year**, you will pay roughly **$20,820** in federal income tax plus FICA in 2026 as a single filer - leaving you with **$79,180 take-home**, or about **$6,598 per month**. Here is the exact bracket-by-bracket math.

## The short answer

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross salary | $100,000 |
| Federal income tax | $13,170 |
| Social Security + Medicare (FICA) | $7,650 |
| **Total tax** | **$20,820** |
| **Take-home pay** | **$79,180** |
| Monthly take-home | $6,598 |
| Effective tax rate | 20.8% |

These numbers assume you are a **single filer** taking the **2026 standard deduction of $16,100**, with no state income tax.

## Federal income tax: $13,170

- $100,000 − $16,100 = **$83,900 taxable income**

| Bracket | Taxable slice | Rate | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0 – $12,400 | $12,400 | 10% | $1,240 |
| $12,401 – $50,400 | $38,000 | 12% | $4,560 |
| $50,401 – $83,900 | $33,500 | 22% | $7,370 |
| **Total** | | | **$13,170** |

Your **marginal rate** is 22% (it stays 22% all the way to $105,700), but your **effective federal rate** is 13.2%. Only the dollars above each threshold are taxed at the higher rate.

## FICA: $7,650

- **Social Security:** 6.2% × $100,000 = $6,200
- **Medicare:** 1.45% × $100,000 = $1,450
- **Total FICA:** $7,650

## $80K vs $100K: where does the extra $20K go?

Going from $80,000 to $100,000 costs you about **$5,930 more in tax** ($20,820 − $14,890) - so you keep roughly **$14,070** of the $20,000 raise. That is a 29.6% marginal bite on the extra income (22% federal + 7.65% FICA), which is exactly how a progressive system should work: you always keep most of a raise.

## What changes the number

1. **State income tax** - the biggest variable. Zero in Texas/Florida; roughly $6,000+ in California at this income.
2. **Pre-tax deductions.** A $23,500 401(k) contribution saves about $5,170 in federal tax alone at the 22% marginal rate.
3. **Filing status.** Married filing jointly doubles the brackets and standard deduction ($32,200).
4. **1099 income.** Freelancers pay self-employment tax instead of half-FICA. Compare with our [1099 vs W-2 calculator](/tools/1099-vs-w2-calculator). ## Married filing jointly: the same $100K costs $5,530 less tax

Filing status changes the answer more than any other single factor. A married couple filing jointly on one $100,000 salary takes a $32,200 standard deduction, leaving $67,800 taxable. The joint brackets are roughly double the single brackets, so the math is:

| | Single filer | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| Standard deduction | $16,100 | $32,200 |
| Taxable income | $83,900 | $67,800 |
| Federal income tax | $13,170 | $7,640 |
| FICA | $7,650 | $7,650 |
| **Total tax** | **$20,820** | **$15,290** |
| **Take-home pay** | **$79,180** | **$84,710** |
| Monthly take-home | $6,598 | $7,059 |
| Biweekly paycheck | $3,045 | $3,258 |

The joint return keeps **$5,530 more** of the same salary, because the wider 10% and 12% brackets shelter more income before the 22% rate starts. If both spouses work, combine both incomes before running these brackets; the joint math above assumes a single $100,000 income for the household.

## Maxing your 401(k): $5,170 less federal tax, $60,850 cash in hand

A $23,500 401(k) contribution in 2026 cuts taxable income from $83,900 to $60,400. Federal tax falls from $13,170 to **$8,000** (10% on $12,400 = $1,240, 12% on $38,000 = $4,560, 22% on the remaining $10,000 = $2,200), a saving of **$5,170**. FICA still applies to the full $100,000, so it stays $7,650. Cash take-home after the contribution and taxes is **$60,850** ($5,071 a month), but $23,500 of that missing cash is now invested for retirement, not lost. That trade is why the 401(k) is usually the first lever to pull before worrying about brackets.

## FAQ

### How much tax will I pay on a $100,000 salary in 2026?
As a single filer taking the standard deduction, about **$20,820** total - $13,170 federal income tax plus $7,650 FICA. Take-home is roughly **$79,180 per year** ($6,598/month) before state taxes.

### What is the take-home pay on $100K salary?
Approximately **$79,180 per year** after federal tax and FICA - about **$6,598/month** or **$3,045 biweekly**. State tax and deductions change this.

### What tax bracket is $100,000 salary in?
**22% marginal** (single filer, 2026), applying only to income above $50,400. Your effective federal rate is about 13.2%.

### How much is FICA on $100K salary?
**$7,650** - $6,200 Social Security plus $1,450 Medicare. You are well under the $184,500 Social Security wage base.

### Should I worry about hitting the next bracket?
The 24% bracket starts at $105,700 of *taxable* income (about $121,800 in salary after the standard deduction). Even if you cross it, only the dollars above the line are taxed at 24%.

### How much tax on $100K salary married filing jointly?
About **$15,290** total: $7,640 federal income tax plus $7,650 FICA. Take-home is roughly **$84,710 per year** ($7,059 a month, $3,258 biweekly), which is $5,530 more than the single-filer take-home of $79,180 on the same salary, because joint brackets are roughly double the single brackets.

### How much tax do I save by maxing my 401(k) on $100K?
A $23,500 401(k) contribution cuts federal tax from $13,170 to **$8,000**, saving **$5,170**. FICA stays $7,650 because it applies to the full salary. Cash take-home after the contribution and taxes is **$60,850** ($5,071 a month), with the $23,500 invested for retirement rather than lost.
