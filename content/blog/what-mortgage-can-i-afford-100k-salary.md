Six figures changes the mortgage conversation, but not as much as most people expect. If you are asking **what mortgage can i afford on 100k salary**, the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule** puts the answer at roughly a **$314,000 to $369,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, depending on whether taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget (they should be). Asked another way, what mortgage can i afford with 100k salary comes down to one monthly number: **$2,333** for all housing costs. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs at or below 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts at or below 36% of gross monthly income

On $100,000 a year ($8,333 a month):

- Front-end limit: $8,333 x 0.28 = **$2,333 a month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $8,333 x 0.36 = **$3,000 a month** for everything

With no other debts, the front-end number binds: $2,333 is your total housing budget, including principal, interest, taxes, insurance and PMI. ## From $2,333 a month to a loan amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $2,333 a month in principal and interest alone supports a loan of about **$369,000**. But that $2,333 must also cover property taxes and insurance, which lenders count as housing. After roughly $350 a month for taxes and insurance, the principal-and-interest budget is closer to $1,983, supporting a loan of about **$314,000**. That gap is why two calculators can give you two different answers on the same salary: one ignores taxes and insurance, the other does not. Trust the lower one.

With 20% down, the $314,000 loan buys roughly a **$392,000 home**. With 10% down it buys roughly a $349,000 home, and with an FHA 3.5% down payment roughly a $325,000 home, before PMI.

## What a $350,000 home actually costs a month

The same house costs very different amounts depending on your down payment, because PMI and the loan size both move:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($12,250) | $337,750 | $2,134.81 | $350 | $154.80 | $2,639.61 |
| 10% ($35,000) | $315,000 | $1,991.01 | $350 | $131.25 | $2,472.26 |
| 20% ($70,000) | $280,000 | $1,769.79 | $350 | $0 | $2,119.79 |

Two lessons here. First, only the 20% down version fits inside the $2,333 guideline. Second, PMI is not a footnote: on the FHA version it adds about $155 a month until you refinance or reach 20% equity. ## Debts shrink the number fast

The back-end ratio is where real buyers lose borrowing power. With debts, the lender uses whichever limit is lower:

| Monthly debts | Back-end housing cap (36% minus debts) | Loan it supports at 6.5%, 30 years, after ~$350 tax and insurance |
|---|---|---|
| $0 | $3,000 (front-end $2,333 binds) | ~$314,000 |
| $800 (car + cards) | $2,200 | ~$293,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $1,500 | ~$182,000 |

On an apples-to-apples basis, $800 a month in debts cuts roughly $21,000 off your loan versus no debts, and $1,500 a month cuts roughly $132,000. Paying off a car before you buy is often worth more than a year of extra saving. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator).

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

Most $100K earners are paid every two weeks: $100,000 / 26 = **$3,846.15 per paycheck** (gross), so the 28% housing budget is **$1,076.92 per paycheck**. Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. On a $280,000 loan at 6.5%, adding $200 a month pays it off in 273 months instead of 360 and saves about **$101,284 in interest**.

## Should you borrow the max?

$2,333 a month is the ceiling, not the target. On $100K gross, take-home is roughly $6,500 a month after federal tax and FICA, before state tax, so a maxed-out payment leaves about $4,200 for everything else. Buying 10 to 15% under your max is the sweet spot between building equity and sleeping well. ## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $100K a year?
Roughly a **$314,000 to $369,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $2,333 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### How much mortgage payment can I afford on $100K salary?
Up to **$2,333 a month** for all housing costs. A $350,000 home with 20% down costs about $2,120 a month, which fits; with 3.5% down it costs about $2,640, which does not.

### Can I afford a $400K house on a $100K salary?
It is a stretch. With 20% down, a $400,000 home costs about $2,422 a month including taxes and insurance, over the $2,333 guideline. You would need roughly a **$104,000 salary** to fit it comfortably at 6.5%.

### How much mortgage can I afford on $100K salary with debts?
With $800 a month in debts, the cap is $2,200 a month and your realistic loan falls to roughly $293,000 after taxes and insurance. With $1,500 a month in debts, the cap is $1,500 and the loan falls to roughly $182,000.

### What is a good down payment on a $100K salary?
20% ($65,000 to $78,000 on a $325,000 to $392,000 home) avoids PMI entirely and is achievable in 2 to 3 years of focused saving at this income. 10% works too, with PMI of roughly $125 to $155 a month until you reach 20% equity.

### Does a $100K salary guarantee mortgage approval?
No. The 28/36 math sets the ceiling, but approval also depends on credit score, employment history, down payment source and the appraisal.
