"Save 3–6 months of expenses" is the most repeated and least explained advice in personal finance. Three to six months of *what*, exactly? And why not two, or twelve? Here is how to size yours properly.

## Start with expenses, not income

The target is months of **essential expenses**, not months of salary. Add up what you must spend in a month if you cut everything optional: housing, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, minimum debt payments, childcare. For many households that is 60–70% of take-home pay.

If your essentials are $3,500/month, a 6-month fund is $21,000. Using income instead of expenses overstates the target and makes it feel impossible, which is why people give up.

## 3 months, 6 months, or more?

- **3 months** is the floor, suitable if you have a stable salaried job, dual incomes, strong job security, or family support to fall back on.
- **6 months** is the standard recommendation for most people: single income household, average job security, no safety net.
- **9–12 months** makes sense if you are self-employed, work on commission, have irregular income, have a chronic health condition, or are the sole earner with dependents.

The right number also depends on **how fast you could replace income**. A nurse in a high-demand market might find work in weeks; a specialized executive might need six months. Be honest about your own situation.

## Where to keep it

An emergency fund must be **safe, liquid, and slightly out of reach**:

- **High-yield savings account (HYSA):** The default best answer. FDIC insured, earns 4–5% in normal rate environments, transfers in 1–3 days. The small delay is actually a feature, it prevents impulse raids.
- **Money market account:** Similar, sometimes with check-writing.
- **NOT invested in stocks:** The market crashes exactly when layoffs spike. An emergency fund that is down 30% when you lose your job is not an emergency fund.
- **NOT in cash at home:** No interest, no insurance, and temptation.

Keep it at a **different bank** from your checking account if you lack discipline. Out of sight, out of mind.

## How to build it from zero

$21,000 sounds impossible from zero. Build it in layers:

1. **Starter: $1,000.** Sell stuff, pause subscriptions, redirect every spare dollar. This covers the flat tire and the ER copay, the emergencies that otherwise become credit card debt.
2. **One month of expenses.** Automate a transfer on payday, even $100/month. Treat it like a bill.
3. **Full target.** Redirect windfalls: tax refunds, bonuses, raises. Bank half of every raise until the fund is full.

While building, **pause non-essential investing** beyond the employer 401(k) match. A 7% market return means nothing if a $2,000 emergency lands on a 24% credit card.

## When to use it (and when not to)

Use it for true emergencies: job loss, medical bills, essential car or home repairs. Do not use it for sales, vacations, or "emergencies" like concert tickets. After any withdrawal, **rebuilding the fund becomes your #1 financial priority** until it is full again.

## The bottom line

Calculate your monthly essentials, multiply by the months that fit your job security, park it in a high-yield savings account, and automate the building.
