If **you make $290K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$991,000-$1,071,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$1,101,000-$1,339,000** range depending on your down payment. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 290k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $991,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $1,071,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$290,000 ÷ 12 = **$24,166.67/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $24,166.67 × 0.28 = **$6,766.67/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $24,166.67 × 0.36 = **$8,700/month**

That $6,766.67 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $6,766.67/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $6,766.67 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$1,071,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $6,766.67 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $6,266.67 → **≈ $991,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$991K-$1,071K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $8,700/month, but on $290K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $1,933 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $7,700, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$991,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $7,200, still just above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$991,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,700 → mortgage drops to roughly **$981,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $6,200 → mortgage roughly **$902,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,700 → mortgage roughly **$823,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $290K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $290,000 ÷ 26 = **$11,153.85**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $6,766.67 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$3,123.08**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $991,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$2,890.98** (the rest of the $3,123.08 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
