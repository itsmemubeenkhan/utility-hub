If **you make $260K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$881,000-$960,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$979,000-$1,200,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 260k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $881,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $960,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$260,000 ÷ 12 = **$21,666.67/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $21,666.67 × 0.28 = **$6,066.67/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $21,666.67 × 0.36 = **$7,800/month**

That $6,066.67 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $6,066.67/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $6,066.67 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$960,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $6,066.67 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$500/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $5,566.67 → **≈ $881,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$881K-$960K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $7,800/month, but on $260K the front-end rule binds first until your debts pass about $1,733 a month:

- **$1,000/month** (car payment): back-end cap is $6,800, above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$881,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** back-end cap is $6,300, still just above your front-end budget, so your mortgage stays roughly **$881,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,800 → mortgage drops to roughly **$839,000**
- **$2,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $5,300 → mortgage roughly **$759,000**
- **$3,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $4,800 → mortgage roughly **$680,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$79,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 260k salary with debts** precisely, here are five debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $6,066.67 | ~$881,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $6,066.67 | ~$881,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $5,800 | ~$839,000 |
| $2,500 (car + loans + cards) | $5,300 | ~$759,000 |
| $3,000 (car + loans + cards) | $4,800 | ~$680,000 |

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $260K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $260,000 ÷ 26 = **$10,000.00**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $6,066.67 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$2,800.00**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $881,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$2,570.09** (the rest of the $2,800.00 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

Roughly a **$881,000 to $960,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $6,066.67 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford a $1M house on a $260K salary?

### Can I afford a $950K house on a $260K salary?
Yes. With 20% down, a $950,000 home costs about **$5,303.72 a month** including taxes and insurance, $762.95 under the $6,066.67 budget. With 10% down it costs about $6,296.06 a month, $229.39 over the guideline.

### What mortgage can I afford on $260K salary with debts?

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $260,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$6,066.67/month** for housing, supporting about a **$881,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $960,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $260K, your housing budget is **$2,800.00 per paycheck** ($10,000.00 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $881,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $2,570.09 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Does a $1,500 monthly car payment reduce what I can borrow on $260K?
No. The back-end cap with $1,500 in debts is $6,300 a month, still above your $6,066.67 front-end budget, so your borrowing power stays at about **$881,000**. Debts only start shrinking it once they pass about $1,733 a month.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
