APR and APY look like alphabet soup, but confusing them costs real money. One tells you what borrowing costs; the other tells you what saving earns. Here is how to never mix them up again.

## APR: the price of borrowing

**APR (Annual Percentage Rate)** is the yearly cost of borrowing, expressed as a percentage. It includes not just the interest rate but also most lender fees, spread across the loan term, so it is the number you use to **compare loans apples to apples**.

A $300,000 mortgage at 6.5% interest with $6,000 in fees might show an APR of 6.72%. A competing offer at 6.625% interest with $1,500 in fees might show an APR of 6.70%, actually cheaper despite the higher rate. **Always compare APR, not the headline rate**, when shopping for mortgages, auto loans, or personal loans.

One catch: APR does *not* account for compounding within the year on most loans, and credit card APRs are notoriously misleading because they assume you carry the balance all year without paying down principal.

## APY: the reward for saving

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield)** is what your savings earn in a year **including compound interest**. It answers: "if I leave $10,000 here for a year, how much will I actually have?"

A savings account advertising 4.00% interest compounded monthly has an APY of about 4.07%. The APY is always equal to or higher than the nominal rate, because compounding adds extra growth. **When comparing savings accounts, CDs, or money market accounts, compare APY**, it is the only number that reflects what you actually earn.

## Side-by-side

| | APR | APY |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Borrowing (loans, cards) | Saving (accounts, CDs) |
| Includes fees | Usually yes | N/A |
| Includes compounding | Usually no | Yes, always |
| Higher is... | Worse for you | Better for you |
| Use it to | Compare loan costs | Compare savings earnings |

Memory trick: **APR = A Price to Repay. APY = A Profit to You.**

## How the confusion costs you

- **Borrowers** who compare interest rates instead of APRs pick loans with low rates but high fees, paying thousands more.
- **Savers** who compare "interest rates" instead of APYs leave money on the table, a 4.5% APY account beats a 4.4% rate compounded annually by real dollars over time.
- **Credit cards** quote APR (often 20%+), but if you carry a balance, the *effective* rate with daily compounding is slightly higher than the stated APR. Pay in full and the APR is irrelevant.

## The bottom line

Borrowing? Look at APR, lower wins. Saving? Look at APY, higher wins. Never compare one against the other.
