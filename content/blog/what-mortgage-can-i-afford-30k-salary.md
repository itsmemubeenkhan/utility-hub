A $30,000 salary can buy a home, but only a small one, and the math leaves almost no slack. If you are asking **what mortgage can i afford on 30k salary**, the lending industry's standard **28/36 rule** puts the answer at roughly a **$87,000 to $111,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, depending on whether taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget (they should be). Asked another way, what mortgage can i afford with 30k salary comes down to one monthly number: **$700** for all housing costs. Here is the exact math.

## The 28/36 rule, step by step

Lenders use two ratios:

- **Front-end (28%):** Housing costs at or below 28% of gross monthly income
- **Back-end (36%):** All monthly debts at or below 36% of gross monthly income

On $30,000 a year ($2,500 a month):

- Front-end limit: $2,500 x 0.28 = **$700 a month** for housing
- Back-end limit: $2,500 x 0.36 = **$900 a month** for everything

With no other debts, the front-end number binds: $700 is your total housing budget, including principal, interest, taxes, insurance and PMI. ## From $700 a month to a loan amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year loan, $700 a month in principal and interest alone supports a loan of about **$111,000**. But that $700 must also cover property taxes and insurance, which lenders count as housing. After roughly $150 a month for taxes and insurance, the principal-and-interest budget is closer to $550, supporting a loan of about **$87,000**. That gap is why two calculators can give you two different answers on the same salary: one ignores taxes and insurance, the other does not. Trust the lower one.

With 20% down, the $87,000 loan buys roughly a **$109,000 home**. With 10% down it buys roughly a $97,000 home, and with an FHA 3.5% down payment roughly a $90,000 home, before PMI.

## What a $100,000 home actually costs a month

Here is the tight truth at this income: a $100,000 home only fits the guideline with 20% down. The same house costs very different amounts depending on your down payment, because PMI and the loan size both move:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($3,500) | $96,500 | $609.95 | $150 | $44.23 | $804.17 |
| 10% ($10,000) | $90,000 | $568.86 | $150 | $37.50 | $756.36 |
| 20% ($20,000) | $80,000 | $505.65 | $150 | $0 | $655.65 |

Only the 20% version fits inside the $700 guideline, with about $44 a month to spare. The 10% version misses by about $56, and the FHA version by about $104. The home that fits exactly with 20% down is closer to **$109,000**: an $87,016 loan costs about $550 in principal and interest, plus $150 in taxes and insurance, for **$700 a month**, right on the line. ## Debts shrink the number fast

The back-end ratio is where real buyers lose borrowing power. With debts, the lender uses whichever limit is lower:

| Monthly debts | Back-end housing cap (36% minus debts) | Loan it supports at 6.5%, 30 years, after ~$150 tax and insurance |
|---|---|---|
| $0 | $900 (front-end $700 binds) | ~$87,000 |
| $200 (car payment) | $700 (same as front-end) | ~$87,000 |
| $400 (car + student loans) | $500 | ~$55,000 |

On an apples-to-apples basis, $200 a month in debts costs nothing extra here because the front-end cap already binds, but $400 a month cuts roughly $32,000 off your loan. At this income, clearing even one monthly payment before you buy can matter more than a year of extra saving. ## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

Most $30K earners are paid every two weeks: $30,000 / 26 = **$1,153.85 per paycheck** (gross), so the 28% housing budget is **$323.08 per paycheck**. Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. On an $87,016 loan at 6.5%, adding $100 a month pays it off in 240 months instead of 360 and saves about **$42,604 in interest**.

## Should you borrow the max?

$700 a month is the ceiling, not the target. On $30K gross, take-home is roughly $2,186 a month after federal tax and FICA, before state tax, so a maxed-out payment leaves about $1,486 for everything else. Buying 10 to 15% under your max is the sweet spot between building equity and sleeping well. Earn a little more? ## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $30K a year?
Roughly a **$87,000 to $111,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $700 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### How much mortgage payment can I afford on $30K salary?
Up to **$700 a month** for all housing costs. A $109,000 home with 20% down costs about $700 a month, which fits exactly; a $100,000 home with 20% down costs about $656, which fits with room to spare.

### Can I afford a $100K house on a $30K salary?
Yes, but only with 20% down: a $100,000 home with $20,000 down costs about **$656 a month** including taxes and insurance, inside the $700 guideline. With 10% down it costs about $756 and with an FHA loan about $804, both over the line.

### Can I afford a $150K house on a $30K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $150,000 home costs about $958 a month including taxes and insurance, well over the $700 guideline. You would need roughly a **$41,000 salary** to fit it comfortably at 6.5%.

### How much mortgage can I afford on $30K salary with debts?
With $200 a month in debts, nothing changes: the back-end cap is $700, the same as the front-end, so your realistic loan stays around $87,000. With $400 a month in debts, the cap falls to $500 and the loan falls to roughly $55,000.

### What is a good down payment on a $30K salary?
20% (about $21,800 on a $109,000 home) avoids PMI entirely, but it is a big sum at this income, so an FHA 3.5% down payment of roughly $3,800 on the same home is the realistic entry path, with PMI of roughly $44 a month. Saving the 20% while paying rent takes most buyers 3 to 5 years.

### Does a $30K salary guarantee mortgage approval?
No. The 28/36 math is only the affordability ceiling. Approval also needs a qualifying credit score, stable employment history, and a debt-to-income ratio under the lender's cap, usually 43 to 45%. A strong credit file and no other debts matter as much as the salary number itself.
