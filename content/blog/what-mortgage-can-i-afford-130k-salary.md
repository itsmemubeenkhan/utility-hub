If **you make $130K a year and wonder what mortgage you can afford**, here is the straight answer: lenders will approve you for a mortgage loan of roughly **$405,000-$480,000** at current rates, which buys a home in the **$445,000-$505,000** range depending on your down payment. Below is the exact math so you can adjust it for your situation. Phrased as **what mortgage can i afford on 130k salary**, the answer is the same range: about a $405,000 loan once taxes and insurance are counted inside the budget, up to $480,000 on principal and interest alone.

## Start with your monthly income

$130,000 ÷ 12 = **$10,833.33/month** gross. Lenders judge you on two ratios, together called the **28/36 rule**:

- **Front-end (28%):** housing costs ≤ 28% of gross monthly income → $10,833.33 × 0.28 = **$3,033.33/month**
- **Back-end (36%):** all monthly debts ≤ 36% of gross monthly income → $10,833.33 × 0.36 = **$3,900/month**

That $3,033.33 is your total housing budget: principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI and HOA - everything.

## Converting $3,033.33/month into a mortgage amount

At 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, every $1,000 borrowed costs about **$6.32/month** in principal and interest. The math:

- **P&I only:** $3,033.33 ÷ $6.32 × $1,000 ≈ **$480,000 mortgage**
- **Reality check:** that $3,033.33 must also cover taxes and insurance (~$475/month on a typical home). Real P&I budget: $2,558.33 → **≈ $405,000 mortgage**

So your realistic mortgage range is **$405K-$480K**. The lower number is what you will actually be approved for once taxes and insurance are counted; the higher number is the theoretical max if taxes and insurance were zero.

## Can you afford a $500,000 house on a $130K salary?

Yes, with 20% down. A $500,000 home at 6.5% on a 30-year loan fits the $3,033.33 monthly housing budget at 20% down, and misses it at lower down payments:

| Down payment | Loan | Principal + interest | Taxes + insurance | PMI (estimate) | Total a month | vs budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHA 3.5% ($17,500) | $482,500 | $3,049.73 | $475 | $201.04 | $3,725.77 | $692.44 over |
| 10% ($50,000) | $450,000 | $2,844.31 | $475 | $168.75 | $3,488.06 | $454.72 over |
| 20% ($100,000) | $400,000 | $2,528.27 | $475 | $0 | $3,003.27 | $30.06 under |

With 20% down, the $500,000 home fits with $30.06 a month to spare. With 10% down it runs $454.72 over the guideline, mostly because of PMI. If you are short on down payment, read [what is PMI and how to avoid it](/blog/what-is-pmi-how-to-avoid) before you decide. To afford a $550,000 home comfortably at 6.5% with 20% down, you would need [roughly a **$140,000 salary**](/blog/what-mortgage-can-i-afford-140k-salary): a $440,000 loan costs $2,781.10 in principal and interest plus $475 in taxes and insurance, or $3,256.10 a month, which needs $11,628.93 a month gross at the 28% rule.

## If you have other debts

This is where most people get tripped up. The back-end rule caps *everything* at $3,900/month:

- **No debts:** full $3,033.33 housing budget → ~$405K-$480K mortgage
- **$1,000/month** (car payment): housing capped at $2,900 → mortgage drops to roughly **$384,000**
- **$1,500/month in debts:** housing capped at $2,400 → mortgage drops to roughly **$305,000**
- **$2,000/month in debts:** housing capped at $1,900 → mortgage roughly **$225,000**

Every $500/month in debt payments erases about **$60,000-$80,000** of mortgage once the back-end rule binds. Paying off a car before house-hunting is one of the highest-leverage moves you can make.

To answer **what mortgage can i afford on 130k salary with debts** precisely, here are three debt levels after taxes and insurance are counted (6.5%, 30-year):

| Monthly debts | Housing cap (36% minus debts) | Realistic mortgage |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 (car payment) | $2,900 | ~$384,000 |
| $1,500 (car + student loans) | $2,400 | ~$305,000 |
| $2,000 (car + loans + cards) | $1,900 | ~$225,000 |

At $2,000 a month in debts your borrowing power is roughly half what it is with no debts. Check your own ratio with our [debt-to-income calculator](/tools/debt-to-income-calculator) before you start house-hunting.

## What about a 15-year mortgage?

On $130K, a 15-year loan is tight but not impossible. At 6.0%, $405,000 over 15 years costs **$3,415.56/month** in P&I alone - already over your $3,033.33 housing budget before taxes and insurance. A smaller 15-year loan of about $359,000 fits ($3,033.33 P&I at 6.0%), but most buyers are better off with the 30-year term and extra principal payments when they can.

## Paid biweekly? Your per-paycheck housing budget

If you are paid every two weeks, your $130K salary breaks down differently, and it helps to think in paychecks instead of months:

- **Gross per paycheck:** $130,000 ÷ 26 = **$5,000.00**
- **Housing budget per paycheck:** $3,033.33 × 12 ÷ 26 = **$1,400.00**
- **P&I per paycheck on a $405,000 loan at 6.5%:** about **$1,180.77** (the rest of the $1,400.00 covers taxes and insurance)

Twice a year you get a third paycheck in the same month. If you route those two extra paychecks' housing share toward principal, you pay the loan off years early without feeling a monthly pinch - the biweekly-payment trick, without a special program.

## FAQ

### What mortgage can I afford on $130K salary?
Roughly a **$405,000 to $480,000 loan** on a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, per the 28/36 rule. The lower end counts taxes and insurance inside the $3,033.33 monthly budget; the higher end is principal and interest only.

### Can I afford a $500K house on a $130K salary?
Yes, with 20% down. A $500,000 home with a $400,000 loan costs about **$3,003.27 a month** including taxes and insurance, $30.06 under the $3,033.33 budget. With 10% down it costs about $3,488.06 a month, $454.72 over the guideline.

### Can I afford a $550K house on a $130K salary?
Not comfortably. With 20% down, a $550,000 home costs about **$3,256.10 a month** including taxes and insurance, $222.77 over the $3,033.33 budget. You would need roughly a **$140,000 salary** to afford it within the 28% guideline at 6.5%.

### What mortgage can I afford on $130K salary with debts?
With $1,000 a month in debts, about **$384,000**. With $1,500 a month in debts, about **$305,000**. With $2,000 a month in debts, about **$225,000**. All figures are after taxes and insurance at 6.5% on a 30-year loan.

### How much house can I afford on $130K with 20% down?
About a **$506,000 home**: a $405,000 loan costs $2,558.33 in principal and interest plus $475 in taxes and insurance, or $3,033.33 a month, right at the 28% guideline. A $500,000 home with 20% down also fits at $3,003.27 a month.

### How much of a mortgage can I afford if I make $130,000 a year with no debt?
With zero other debts, the front-end ratio is the binding limit: **$3,033.33/month** for housing, supporting about a **$405,000 mortgage** after taxes and insurance (or $480,000 P&I-only).

### I get paid biweekly - how much mortgage can I afford per paycheck?
On $130K, your housing budget is **$1,400.00 per paycheck** ($5,000.00 gross per paycheck, 28% rule). A $405,000 loan at 6.5% takes about $1,180.77 of that in principal and interest, leaving room for taxes and insurance.

### Is the 28/36 rule a law?
No - it is a guideline most conventional lenders follow. FHA loans allow up to 31/43, and some lenders stretch further, but borrowing above these ratios sharply increases the risk of becoming house poor.
