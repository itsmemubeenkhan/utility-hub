Tipping movers confuses people because there is no bill with a percentage printed on it. The working rule: tip **per mover, per hour** for local moves, roughly **$5 to $10 an hour each**, and scale up for a full day, heavy items or difficult access. This guide turns that rule into exact amounts for the common job types.

## Quick amounts by job type

| Job type | Tip per mover | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small local move (2 to 3 hours) | $20 to $30 | Per mover, flat |
| Half-day local move (4 to 5 hours) | $30 to $50 | About $7.50/hr at the midpoint |
| Full-day local move (8+ hours) | $40 to $60 | More for stairs or heavy items |
| Long-distance move | $100 to $250 total for the loading crew | 5% to 10% of a smaller move bill, split |
| Piano, safe or hot tub add-on | Add $10 to $20 each | On top of the base tip |

## The hourly math, worked out

For a **half-day local move**: 3 movers, 5 hours, a mid-range $7.50 per hour each: 5 × $7.50 = **$37.50 per mover**, so **$112.50 total** for the crew. Rounding to $40 each ($120) is what most people do, and it is well within the normal band.

For a **full day**: 2 movers who spend 9 hours loading, driving and unloading earn **$50 each** at a typical good-service level, $100 total. Difficult conditions (a fourth-floor walk-up, a piano, summer heat) justify $60 each.

## Percentage method: when it works and when it does not

Some guides suggest 15% to 20% of the moving bill, like a restaurant. That breaks down on real invoices: 15% of a $4,000 full-service move is $600, far above what crews expect, while 15% of a $400 labor-only job is only $60 for a full crew. Percentage tipping makes sense mainly for **long-distance moves priced as one bill**: on a $5,000 move, 5% is $250 and 10% is $500, reasonable for a crew that loaded over a full day. For local hourly jobs, the per-hour-per-mover rule tracks effort far better. The same logic applies at restaurants in reverse; see [how much to tip at a bar](/blog/how-much-to-tip-bartender) for how percentage and per-item rules trade places.

## What should move the amount up or down

**Tip more when:** there are stairs or a long carry from truck to door; items are unusually heavy or fragile; the crew packs for you; the weather is brutal; they finish ahead of schedule without damage.

**Tip less, or speak to the company first, when:** the crew arrived hours late without notice, items were damaged, or the final bill jumped far beyond the written estimate. Tipping is for service quality; billing disputes belong with the office, not taken silently out of the crew's tips if the crew itself worked well.

## Cash, per person, at the end

Hand the tip to **each mover individually** rather than giving a lump sum to the driver, so it is split as you intended. Cash is still king on moving day, though many crews accept Venmo or Cash App. Tip at the end of the job once the last item is placed, and for long-distance moves tip the loading crew and the delivery crew separately, since they are usually different people.

Budget the tip when you budget the move itself; our [50/30/20 budget rule guide](/blog/50-30-20-budget-rule) is a good way to slot moving costs into the "wants vs needs" side of a monthly plan, and an [emergency fund](/blog/emergency-fund-how-much) is what keeps a move from becoming credit card debt.

## What about food and drinks?

Offering water, sports drinks and a pizza lunch is a well-liked gesture on a full-day job and costs little, but it is a **bonus, not a substitute** for the cash tip. Crews consistently say they would rather have the money.

## FAQ

### How much do you tip movers for a local move?
Budget $5 to $10 per hour per mover. A 3-person crew on a 5-hour move at the $7.50 midpoint is $37.50 each, $112.50 total. Small 2 to 3 hour jobs usually land at $20 to $30 per mover flat.

### Should I tip movers 20% like a restaurant?
Not for local moves. Percentages break on large invoices: 20% of a $4,000 move is $800, far above norms. Percentages only make sense on long-distance bills, where 5% to 10% ($250 to $500 on a $5,000 move) is typical for the loading crew.

### Do I tip each mover separately?
Yes. Hand each crew member their own tip so the split matches your intention. For a long-distance move, tip the loading crew at pickup and the (usually different) delivery crew at drop-off.

### How much extra should I tip for stairs or a piano?
Add roughly $10 to $20 per mover on top of the base tip for a piano, gun safe, hot tub or a multi-flight walk-up. These jobs carry real injury risk and extra time.

### Is it okay not to tip movers?
Tipping is customary in the US but not mandatory. If service was genuinely poor, say damaged items or extreme lateness, address it with the company. For competent service, skipping the tip entirely is considered poor form; the per-hour rule keeps it proportional.

### Should I tip in cash or through the company?
Cash (or a payment app) directly to each mover is best. If you add a tip to a card payment through the office, ask how and when it reaches the crew, since splits and timing vary. Work out a fair total beforehand with the [tip calculator](/tools/tip-calculator).
